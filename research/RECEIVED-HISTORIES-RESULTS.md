# A sequence reaches an untouched receiver

The selected distant receiver passes the locked order-resolution gate. All 24
orders of the same four source contacts have distinct recorded replies at both
timesteps, after the connections and transient state are removed. Every finer-
timestep reply identifies its own coarse-timestep history as the unique nearest
response. Erasing both retained fields gives the fresh isolated control exactly.

The nearer receiver also distinguishes all 24 orders at each timestep, but only
18 of its 24 finer responses select their own coarse history. The remaining six
select a different one. This is a difference between two chosen observations in
this chain; it does not establish a general advantage of distance or a causal
explanation for the difference.

## Fixed question and execution revision

`RECEIVED-HISTORIES-PROTOCOL.md` was committed before execution. The complete
universe is 24 permutations of four fixed contacts A-D. Locations, amplitudes,
polarity and envelopes are unchanged across orders. The contacts fill the same
four time slots. The source writes for 30 seconds; two untouched receivers are
then separated and reset to their own retained rest shape, with velocity, delay
and phase cleared. Both receive the identical 12-second, three-note question.
The metric includes all twelve pitch readouts at 289 times, including modes
that are not directly excited by that question.

The first eight-chain implementation failed its declared numerical admission:
bridge-velocity disagreement was 6.735325e-6 against a 2e-6 limit. Body-field
errors were below 9.84e-7 and pitch error was zero, but that did not waive the
failed bridge check. No order evaluation ran in that first directory.

`RECEIVED-HISTORIES-REVISION-001.md` then rejected this packing. Each writing
history ran alone, with the original gestures, precision, thresholds and
scientific criteria unchanged. Its six-second admission reproduces every saved
single-chain reference field, bridge value and pitch exactly. The failed packed
run and both versions of the producing source remain in the record.

## Field interventions

Both-erased replies match the fresh control exactly for every history, receiver
and rate. Partial erasure leaves different, non-additive observations. At the
distant receiver, erasing inscription lowers the minimum pairwise distance to
0.000466635 Hz at 96 steps/second and 0.000400690 Hz at 192. Erasing wear leaves
minima 0.001985577 and 0.001981075 Hz. The cross-timestep order gate was specified
for the connected retained state, and is not silently reused as an admission
claim about these partial conditions.

The independent verifier hashes 164 files, reconstructs all 768 retained-field
interventions, checks that every probe begins with u=p and zero velocity,
delay, phase and clock, and recomputes all within-rate and cross-rate distances.
Every recomputed matrix agrees exactly in the saved NumPy comparison; all
summaries and labels pass reconstruction. The verifier does not independently
reimplement the generating dynamics.

## Interpretation

The original transfer study asks whether an encounter leaves a changed reply.
This follow-up shows that its selected distant receiver also resolves the order
of four fixed writing ingredients under the declared numerical reader and
timestep comparison. The second receiver's more consistent matching in this
specific comparison makes the observer part of the work's subject.

The closest differences are small. No human discrimination, pressure/noise
robustness, physical storage capacity, continuum convergence or infinite-time
arithmetic rank is established. Equal prescribed contacts do not imply equal
realized mechanical work. The result covers this entire selected four-token
universe and this finite material; it is not a sample of all possible gestures.
