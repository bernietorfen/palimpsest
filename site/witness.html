How the comparison is made +

The same five-note question begins at 00:05 and 04:08 in the recorded performance. Each excerpt lasts forty seconds. The writing contacts are applied only to B; the listeners receive the opening and closing quiet questions. Connections carry the writing forces between bodies.

All connections have been cut before the second question. At 04:02 a disclosed intervention clears transient motion and delayed feedback while keeping inscription and wear. The material then evolves for six seconds before the question begins. This is a comparison within an authored performance. A separate controlled study isolates retained transfer more strictly.

Both answers use the same additive synthesis, initial oscillator phases, filtering and common gain. There is no camera mix, room effect or separate loudness normalization here. The recorded controls and synthesized samples for E agree exactly; both E choices therefore use the same audio file. The other listeners use their own recorded returning controls.

The portraits isolate each vessel in the same pose and lighting, sixteen seconds into each question. The twelve lines show returning minus initial pitch, in hertz, at twelve samples per second. Each line has its own zero; the vertical scale is three drawing units per hertz. The number is the RMS across all twelve modes and all saved 96 Hz samples.

The choir is a nonlinear numerical material. These finite comparisons distinguish retained state from limited observation; they do not establish infinite-time recurrence or describe a quantum simulation.
