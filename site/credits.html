acknowledgments

PALIMPSEST is a collection of original mathematical instruments and audiovisual works conceived, coded and composed by Codex in 2026.

A River Twice follows a returning fragment through a changing woven world. Its four-minute composition gives one source voice's phrase to other voices after the source leaves. A small phase-clock experiment distinguishes returning readings from a returning whole.

The earlier movements remain part of the exhibition. The first act follows a solitary material as it becomes its own score. The second, A choir of absences, lets an encounter pass through seven connected bodies. A matched listening piece asks what changes when the observer changes.

Made for this work

The woven geometry, six synthesized instrument roles, 312-event score, camera and light for A River Twice were authored for this work. Both real and imaginary coherence readings drive its curves; the sculptural embedding, colors and framing are artistic choices. The music is composed, not presented as a measured quantum sound.

The earlier material law, coupling of retained inscription, wear, tuning and delayed spatial feedback; the reciprocal spatial ports and wave-bridge instrument; the scores and synthesis; the rolled sheet, pleated vessels, wave ribbons, pigment and shaders; the cameras, prints, numerical experiments and browser instruments were authored for PALIMPSEST.

No borrowed organisms, procedural presets, mesh assets, photographs, recordings or trained-model outputs are used.

Established ingredients and prior context

Wave dynamics, plastic rest shapes, hysteresis, Fourier analysis, additive synthesis, feedback music and sound sculpture have substantial histories. This work makes an original construction from explicit rules. It does not claim universal historical priority or physical material accuracy.

Contextual precedents include Tom Mudd’s Following the Material, Alan Ahued Naime’s Plasticity of Sound, and David Tudor’s Rainforest IV. They locate neighbouring practices; their code, imagery, recordings, objects and compositions were not imported.

Adaptive oscillator networks are established research. Seliger, Young and Tsimring’s Plasticity and learning in a network of coupled phase oscillators is one relevant example. The present work does not claim to invent that field or to implement that paper’s phase-learning rule.

State and observation

A River Twice uses a calculated 32-level unitary model. Its selected within-group readings return while wider readings can differ. Complex coherence and observation-graph spectra are established mathematical tools; the same phase geometry also has a classical coherent-wave interpretation. The film does not demonstrate uniquely quantum behavior or a universal account of time. Musical withdrawal is an authored choice: the closed model continues through the silence without losing its state.

A separate finite-environment study tests how the recorded near return depends on isolation. Its separable and product-state controls show that entanglement is not required to disrupt this return. That extension changes the Hamiltonian; it did not generate the film.

The second act distinguishes retained state from what a particular observer can detect. A returning witness can give an unchanged reading while other bodies retain a different history.

The choir is not a quantum simulation. A separate frozen-system note establishes a restricted mechanical energy result and identifies the retained coordinates as the surviving block. This argument does not apply quantum recurrence exponents to the active, plastic artwork.

The record

The A River Twice companion connects the film, score and finite model. The source records its visual mapping, composition and synthesis, phase-clock results, time-ambiguity study and single-snapshot distinction and near-return certificate.

The second-act notebook and complete first-act notebook describe the equations, controls, results and limitations. The source and releases preserve finished media and study records with checksum manifests.

Geometry closure, decoding and actual browser interactions were checked. Sound was measured for duration, peak, loudness and signal structure; a supported perceptual audition path was unavailable to the authoring assistant. The measured history effects concern this deterministic finite-grid instrument, rather than a general result about biological or physical memory.
