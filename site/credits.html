Authorship &
acknowledgments

PALIMPSEST was conceived, coded and composed by Codex on 3–4 October 2026. It is an invented mathematical instrument and an original audiovisual work in two acts.

The first act follows a solitary material as it becomes its own score. The second, A choir of absences, lets an encounter pass through seven connected bodies. A matched listening piece asks what changes when the observer changes.

Made for this work

The particular material law, coupling of retained inscription, wear, tuning and delayed spatial feedback; the reciprocal spatial ports and wave-bridge instrument; the two scores and synthesis; the rolled sheet, pleated vessels, wave ribbons, pigment and shaders; the cameras, prints, numerical experiments and browser instruments were authored for PALIMPSEST.

No borrowed organisms, procedural presets, mesh assets, photographs, recordings or trained-model outputs are used.

Established ingredients and prior context

Wave dynamics, plastic rest shapes, hysteresis, Fourier analysis, additive synthesis, feedback music and sound sculpture have substantial histories. This work makes an original construction from explicit rules. It does not claim universal historical priority or physical material accuracy.

Contextual precedents include Tom Mudd’s Following the Material, Alan Ahued Naime’s Plasticity of Sound, and David Tudor’s Rainforest IV. They locate neighbouring practices; their code, imagery, recordings, objects and compositions were not imported.

Adaptive oscillator networks are established research. Seliger, Young and Tsimring’s Plasticity and learning in a network of coupled phase oscillators is one relevant example. The present work does not claim to invent that field or to implement that paper’s phase-learning rule.

State and observation

The second act distinguishes retained state from what a particular observer can detect. A returning witness can give an unchanged reading while other bodies retain a different history.

The choir is not a quantum simulation. A separate frozen-system note establishes a restricted mechanical energy result and identifies the retained coordinates as the surviving block. This argument does not apply quantum recurrence exponents to the active, plastic artwork.

Tools and type

Production uses Python, PyTorch, NumPy, SciPy, ModernGL/OpenGL, Pillow, FFmpeg, Matplotlib, trimesh and ReportLab. The playable instruments use authored JavaScript, WebGL2, workers and Web Audio. General software and mathematical methods retain their respective authorship. Simulation, synthesis, rendering, analysis and browser verification ran on RunPod.

Typography uses DejaVu Serif and DejaVu Sans. The font license accompanies the self-hosted type files.

The record

The second-act notebook and complete first-act notebook describe the equations, controls, results and limitations. The source and releases preserve finished media and study records with checksum manifests.

Geometry closure, decoding and actual browser interactions were checked. Sound was measured for duration, peak, loudness and signal structure; a supported perceptual audition path was unavailable to the authoring assistant. The measured history effects concern this deterministic finite-grid instrument, rather than a general result about biological or physical memory.
