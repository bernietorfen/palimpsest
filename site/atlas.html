Reading the marks

Four moments.
Twelve directions.

The directions around each glyph correspond to the instrument’s twelve voices. From the inside outward, the rings show the start of the probe, then 4, 8 and 12 seconds.

A ring moves outward where that voice’s pitch is higher than the average of all 120 histories at the same moment. It moves inward where the pitch is lower. All glyphs use one scale. The smooth curves are an authored way of drawing those measurements.

What the experiment establishes

All 120 writing orders leave different numerical pitch trajectories. Swapping only the first two gestures still changes the later answer, even with the same last three gestures.

The result survives halving the simulation timestep: each finer-step answer is closest to its own history among the 120 coarser-step records. Erasing both retained fields restores the fresh answer exactly.

These are measurements of this invented instrument. Numeric differences do not establish that every pair can be distinguished by ear. The comparison metric includes all twelve voices, including voices not being directly excited. The audio comes from the 1/96-second run.
