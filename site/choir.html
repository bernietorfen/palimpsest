An instrument made of encounters

What belongs to a voice
after it has been heard?

Each vessel begins with the same unwritten material. The history comes from what you do, where you touch, and what the connections carry. The pigment and the folds follow the material’s retained fields. The sound follows its changing tuning.

Inside the choir +

Seven numerical materials run together. Each bridge is a chain of sixteen masses and reciprocal springs. Its endpoints read a spatial patch of each vessel and return force through that same patch. The displayed ribbon follows the chain’s displacement; its colour follows instantaneous motion.

This playable version uses a 32 × 32 grid for each body and a fixed 1/96-second timestep. A slow device slows the material clock. A worker runs the equations; the scene and sound read its state. The browser calculation was checked against an independent double precision studio calculation at the same resolution.

The pleated vessels, lighting and spatial arrangement are original artistic interpretations of these fields. Their holes depict accumulated wear; they are not a fracture simulation. The bridges’ drawn lengths are not calibrated physical lengths. Cutting or joining a bridge changes the system externally. Rejoining creates a still bridge between the current endpoints.

The voices are synthesized from the twelve material modes in each body. Solo changes only what you hear. Separating a voice cuts its actual connections. Saved encounters keep all material fields, delayed feedback, bridge motion and connection strengths. A recent copy stays in this tab; nothing is uploaded.

The work pauses when this tab is hidden. The guided encounter creates a new choir and preserves your previous state behind “Return to the previous encounter.” It is an authored demonstration, not a scientific control experiment.
